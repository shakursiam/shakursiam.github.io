Research Statement

Thirty years ago, computers filled whole rooms. Better chip design made them small enough for everyone to use. Today, AI is going through a similar shift. It is moving away from large, power-hungry cloud servers. It is moving onto small, low-power devices at the edge.

My research brings together four connected areas. I design custom hardware, such as circuit boards and sensor nodes, built for real-world use. I build lightweight AI models (TinyML) that run on this hardware using very little power. I apply this work to wearable biomedical devices — sensors that can track health signals like heart activity and vital signs. I also study how to keep AI-powered IoT (AIoT) networks secure, so that sensor and health data stays private and protected from attacks.

In my Ph.D., I want to bring these four areas together. My goal is to build wearable and embedded AI systems that are low-power, reliable, and secure — systems that can be trusted in real medical and infrastructure settings.
