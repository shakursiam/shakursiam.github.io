Md Abdus Shakur Siam

Research Assistant, HIVE Research Group, Dept. of EEE, BUET

Dhaka, Bangladesh

I am an Electronics & Embedded Systems Engineer and Research Assistant at the HIVE (Hydrogen, Intelligent Vehicle & Energy) Research Group, Dept. of EEE, Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), working under Dr. Muhammad Anisuzzaman Talukder. I also serve as a Research Associate (Consultancy) at the Infrastructure Investment Facilitation Company (IIFC), contributing to a national scientific inventory assessment report for the Ministry of Finance.

My interest in embedded systems started in my third year at Ahsanullah University of Science and Technology (AUST), when a classmate and I formed a small student team, AUST Rocketry, to compete in the model rocket category at Teknofest-23. As Avionics Sub-system Lead under Prof. Dr. Omar Farrok, I worked with microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, and control systems for the first time — and that project led directly into my undergraduate thesis on the flight control system of a subsonic sounding rocket for meteorological particle observation, where I also learned to design PCBs. I carried that hardware foundation into industry at Frontech Limited, building the JRC AgriTech Board, a custom controller for farm machinery that senses soil moisture in real time.

At BUET, I’ve drawn on that hardware background for battery health prognosis: I proposed and built a charger that charges a battery and predicts its health at the same time, designed around a BQ24610 buck charger and an ESP32-S3 controller. I trained a lightweight State-of-Health / Remaining-Useful-Life model across the NASA, Oxford, MIT, and CALCE battery datasets, tested it on chemistries it had never seen, then quantized the best-performing model and deployed it directly on the charger itself — validated on two commercial cells over 14 charge cycles with under 2.4% prediction error (manuscript in preparation, Elsevier Journal of Energy Storage). Alongside this, I led a review of 168 papers on security and privacy in AI-based IoT systems, and I currently research sound-source localization for a government-backed traffic-horn detection system. I’ve also worked remotely as a hardware/embedded engineer for companies in the UK and Germany — one of the devices I helped design is now sold commercially in the UK (see Projects).

This combination of hardware design, machine learning, and systems thinking is what draws me toward my intended research direction: edge AI, in-sensor computing, cyber-physical systems, and wearable IoT devices — building full sensing-and-inference pipelines that fit onto a single low-power board, small and efficient enough to live on the body, in the field, or inside a battery.

I completed my B.Sc. in EEE at AUST in 2023, where I also served as Vice President of the AUST Innovation & Design Club — managing 90+ volunteers — and co-initiated the university’s Satellite Research Lab (see Projects for both).

I am currently targeting fully-funded PhD positions (ECE/CS) for Spring/Fall 2027 entry, in the US and abroad.

selected publications

  1. J. Energy Storage
    Edge-Deployed Battery State-of-Health Estimation on a Custom Synchronous Buck Charging Platform: A Cross-Dataset Lightweight Machine Learning Framework
    Md. Abdus Shakur Siam and Muhammad Anisuzzaman Talukder
    Journal of Energy Storage (Elsevier), 2026
    Under final review (internal/editorial) — full text not yet public
    Abs Bib

    Despite advances in battery prognostics, a persistent gap remains between algorithmically accurate machine learning (ML) models and their practical deployment on resource-constrained battery management systems (BMS). This work addresses these gaps via the Edge-SBC, a custom hardware platform integrating a BQ24610 charge controller with an ESP32-S3 host for simultaneous precision charging and on-device ML inference. Benchmarking a zero-data-leakage pipeline across four public datasets yields within-dataset State of Health (SoH) R2 up to 0.9992. A systematic 32-experiment cross-dataset protocol identifies protocol affinity, rather than chemistry, as the dominant transfer factor (leave-one-dataset-out R2 ceiling of 0.761). For deployment, a Float32-downcasted HistGradientBoosting model achieves 52 microseconds latency and <=21 microjoules energy within a 500 KB Flash budget. Live Physical-in-the-Loop (PhIL) validation on two differing 18650 cells (NMC and LCO) demonstrates operational feasibility, achieving optimal mean absolute SoH errors of 1.80% (NMC, 60-minute window) and 2.38% (LCO, end-of-charge).

  2. JNCA
    Privacy and Secure Networking in AI-enabled Internet of Things Systems: A Review
    Md Abdus Shakur Siam, Shamik Dey, Muhammad Imran Ali, and 2 more authors
    Journal of Network and Computer Applications, 2026
    Under final review (internal/editorial) — full text not yet public
    Abs Bib

    Federated intrusion detection systems routinely exceed 99% accuracy on IoT benchmarks, yet only three of 168 reviewed studies validate on live or physical-testbed traffic, and a white-box adversarial attack achieves 100% success against a high-accuracy deep learning classifier. This survey synthesises 168 studies (2019-2026) on AI-specific threat vectors, privacy-preserving mechanisms, and secure networking for IoT within a four-dimensional taxonomy: system layer, AI lifecycle stage, threat category, and AI paradigm class. We derive seven deployment-readiness requirements, mapped to NIST AI RMF 1.0, ISO/IEC 42001:2023, and IEEE Std 2413-2019, and argue that adversarial stress-testing, formal privacy quantification, and governance alignment must become mandatory evaluation standards.

  3. Energy & Buildings
    Multi-Seasonal Assessment of Nanostructured Cool Paints for Passive Radiative Cooling in Tropical Climates Using IoT Sensor Networks
    Md. Abdus Shakur Siam, Md. Fahim Hossain, Ahmed Imtiaz, and 2 more authors
    Energy and Buildings (Elsevier), 2026
    Manuscript complete, pending submission (internal/editorial) — full text not yet public. Industry-funded by Berger Paints Bangladesh Ltd.
    Abs Bib

    Rapid urbanisation and intensifying urban heat island effects are escalating building cooling loads in tropical monsoon (Koppen Am) cities. This study evaluates a dual-component nanostructured cool paint system through a seven-month continuous IoT field campaign (November 2024-May 2025) on three pilot enclosures in Dhaka, Bangladesh, using a custom ESP32/DHT22 sensor network, supplemented by controlled laboratory infrared step-heating chamber testing. The cool-paint system delivered a peak midday internal temperature reduction of 4.15 degC (10.74%) at the April pre-monsoon maximum, with the NOAA Heat Index suppressed by  5 degC under peak pre-monsoon loading. Scaled to a four-storey residential building, the system yielded a base-case net electrical saving of 164.3 kWh and a carbon abatement of 112.0 kg CO2 over six months.
